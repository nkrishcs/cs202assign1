The Wonder Hiking Trails of the Midwest

The Driftless Area is a distinct geographic sanctuary spanning southwestern Wisconsin and adjacent corners of Minnesota, Iowa, and Illinois. While colossal continental glaciers bulldozed and flattened much of the Midwest during the last ice age, this pocket was miraculously spared. Because ice sheets never crushed this region, ancient waterways carved through layers of soft sandstone and limestone over millions of years, forging deeply cut river coulees and dramatic, towering blufftops that rise hundreds of feet above the Mississippi River plain.

Today, the Driftless terrain offers some of the most challenging and visually rewarding hiking in the entire region. Hikers can ascend narrow goat prairies, climb wooded ravines filled with rare wildflowers, and look out over panoramic vistas extending across three states. You can learn more about the natural history and preservation of this landscape through the Driftless Area Land Conservancy.

Grandad Bluff Trail

Grandad Bluff Park — La Crosse, Wisconsin

Overview

Grandad Bluff is the iconic symbol of the Coulee Region as it raises 600 feet above La Crosse, . The trails offer jaw-dropping views of Wisconsin, Minnesota, and Iowa. This route is rated as Moderate Deficulty due to rocky, uneven paths along ledges.

Getting There

Trailhead: 3020 Grandad Bluff Road, La Crosse, WI 54601

  1. From downtown La Crosse, take Main Street eastward toward the bluffs.
  2. Continue up the winding ascent on Grandad Bluff Road:
    1. Pass the lower quarry turnoff.
    2. Take the sharp curve near the top gate.
  3. Park in the upper paved lot adjacent to the stone shelter.

Trail Facts

Trail Length
1.4 miles out-and-back
Elevation Gain
280 feet
Trail Surface
Crushed limestone and exposed bedrock
Dog Policy
Permitted on a 6-foot leash

Miller Bluff Trail

Hixon Forest Reserve — La Crosse, Wisconsin

Overview

Inside the Hixon Forest trail system, Miller Bluff provides a peaceful, shaded hike through hardwood forests and native prairies. With gentle inclines and well-graded singletrack, this route is categorized as Easy Difficulty, making it ideal for families and trail runners.

Getting There

Trailhead: 2700 Milson Avenue, La Crosse, WI 54601

  1. Follow Highway 16 to the Milson Avenue turnoff.
  2. Proceed east straight toward the base of the bluff:
    1. Pass the lower parking lot entrance.
    2. Continue past the nature trail loop head.
  3. Begin your hike by heading east on the Bicentennial Trail junction.

Trail Facts

Trail Length
2.1 miles round trip
Elevation Gain
195 feet
Trail Surface
Packed dirt, grass, and forest loam
Dog Policy
Permitted on a leash and clean-up bags required

Perrot Ridge Trail

Perrot State Park — Trempealeau, Wisconsin

Overview

Perrot Ridge climbs steeply along the sharp spine of a dolomitic bluff where the Trempealeau and Mississippi rivers converge. Due to precipitous cliff drops, wooden step climbs, and steep grades, this hike is rated as Strenuous Difficulty.

Getting There

Trailhead: 9998 State Park Road, Trempealeau, WI 54661

  1. Enter Perrot State Park through the main contact station off County Road K.
  2. Follow State Park Road to the trail staging area:
    1. Pass the campground registration booth.
    2. Turn right into the Upper Picnic Area lot.
  3. Look for the wooden trailhead kiosk on the north side of the parking lot.

Trail Facts

Trail Length
3.2-mile loop
Elevation Gain
510 feet
Trail Surface
Natural stone steps, rock scramble, and dirt
Dog Policy
Permitted on a leash; not recommended for older dogs

Important Packing Checklist

  • Clothes
    • Sturdy hiking boots with good grip
    • Synthetic socks
  • Food and water
    • At least 2 liters of drinking water per person
    • High-energy snacks such as nuts, dried fruit, or jerky
  • Trail Safety
    • Topographic park map and magnetic compass
    • Small first-aid kit with tick tweezers and antiseptic wipes

SAFETY WARNINGS!!!

The high cliffs of the Driftless Area are fragile and unstable. Stay at least six feet back from cliff ledges, as sudden rockfall or slippery moss can cause severe falls. Likewise, open south-facing goat prairies provide prime summer habitat for timber rattlesnakes wihch could be dangerous. Watch your foot placement on rocky ledges, keep pets strictly leashed, and give all wildlife space while hiking.

Glossary

Coulee
A deep, steep-sided valley or ravine sculpted into sandstone and limestone by running water, typical throughout southwestern Wisconsin
Goat Prairie
Hill Prairie
A dry, native grassland that thrives exclusively on steep, south-to-southwest-facing bluff sides where thin soil, harsh sunlight, and wind prevent tree growth.
Switchback
A sharp, zigzagging path constructed up a steep incline to reduce the slope gradient and prevent severe trail erosion.
Karst
Topography formed from the dissolution of soluble bedrock like limestone and dolomite, featuring sinkholes, hidden caves, and cold-water springs.
Talus
A sloping mass of loose rock debris and fallen boulder fragments accumulated at the base of a cliff or steep bluff face.
